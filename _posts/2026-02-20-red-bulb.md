# Preface

This is a fictional story on how innovations happen at a fictional company called "The Bulb Company".

# Chapter 1: The Bright Idea

"The Bulb Company" is an industry leader in producing bulbs. Currently, the world only has seen white-colored bulbs.
Most of these white bulbs are made by "The Bulb Company".

One fine day, the CEO of the Bulb Company comes up with an idea: Why don't we produce colored bulbs? In particular why don't we produce red colored bulbs.
After discussing with his board, he calls the press and makes a big announcement: 
"The Bulb Company is the world leader in producing bulbs. The world currently has not seen colored bulbs. We at the Bulb Company are constantly innovating and
producing new products. In 3 months, we are launching a new product: The Red Bulb."

# Chapter 2: Red Filament

The CEO then calls a meeting with his senior engineers and asks them to come up with a plan to produce 1 million red bulbs in 3 months.
The senior engineers are confused. They say our bulbs are simple. They have a filament in a glass box. The filament glows white when electricity is passed through it.
For the bulb to glow red, we would need a new filament that glows in red. The CEO says that is perfect. He tells all his senior engineers to go look for the "red filament".

One month passes. The senior engineers work hard day and night. They search through every material known to them. None of them seems to glow red. One of them found a filament that glows red, but it burnt away in a few seconds. It didn't seem like they could find a filament that works reasonably well. They all meet again with the CEO to share the disappointing news that they could not find the filament.

# Chapter 3: Why not the Glass

The CEO starts brainstorming ideas with the team. One of the senior engineers suggests: "If not for the filament, what about the box?" Why don't we find a glass that can switch colors? Something that becomes red in color when electricity passes or when it becomes hot and then goes back to being transparent when cooled down. The senior engineer had seen glass working plants that work with molten glass and had seen that molten glass has a bright reddish-orange color. The CEO liked the idea. The group spends the next month to see if they can alter the property of glass using heat, electricity etc. without cracking or melting it up.

# Chapter 4: Back to Basics

Another month passes. The senior engineers meet with the CEO to say this too was a failure. They could not find a glass that can change colors. The group continues brain storming. After some discussions on learnings from all the failed attempts, one senior engineer gets up and gives a bold idea: "Why don't we just paint the glass red?" The CEO likes the idea. The CEO says this a good idea, but would be a bad PR when customers find that these are plain old bulbs painted red. The bulbs cant be sold as such. The CEO thinks for a minute and says, let us intead create something like a "mini-torch" something that shoots a beam of read light where the bulb is hidden inside a black box. Inside this black box, the bulb can be the red-painted white bulb. We can also patent the design, so other don't copy our strategic design.

# Chapter 5: Scaling the vision

With the design finalized, the meeting is considered a success. The senior engineer then takes the design to a team of junior engineers. He says: "we have got fresh work. We are on a high-impact project targetting release in the next month. We are going to be producing red bulbs". The junior engineers are curious now. The senior engineer shows them a sack of million bulbs and tins of red pain. He asks them to paint the million bulbs each with red paint in the coming week, so that he can send it for review. The junior engineers panick. They say that they are 5 people each with 2 hands. 10 hands painting 1 million bulbs within a week is impossible. The senior engineer understands and says take 2 weeks then. But still the junior engineers refuse.

One of the junior engineers comes up with a plan. Why not pour all tins of the paint in a huge bath tub and dunk all the bulbs in the bath tub. We can then let it dry for the next week or two. All the juniors cheer. They say that would work. But the senior engineer does not agree. He says, there is a problem you see. The paint layer would not be uniform around the bulb. The bulbs might not pass the review. That would not be acceptable. But he also understands the problem of the crew.

The junior engineer comes up with a modified plan. He says let us divide our customers in tiers. Premium customers who are loyal to our brand or shop in our premium outlets, or make frequent purchases in general are in the first tier. These are a small number of customers and would get the hand-painted bulbs. This tier interestingly also covers the executives. The second tier includes all other customers. The customers in second tier would get bulbs dunked in the paint tub and dried, possibly unevely. the senior engineer likes this idea. He gets it approved by his superiors. The work starts. The bulbs get ready in 1 week, ahead of the 2 week schedule. It passes the review since the bulbs of tier-2 customers now have a lower bar, justfied by their lowwer business potential.

# Chapter 6: It's a success or is it? 

The bulbs hit the market. It is a grand success. One million bulbs get sold. There is record sales and people's homes are lit up with colored bulbs. 

Months pass. The paint on the bulbs slowly start pealing away as they are exposed to heat. The CEO holds a press conference and announces that the first version of red bulbs were a big success, but they are receptive to customer concerns, about the bulbs loosing their red color over time. The CEO announces that red bulb v2 will fix all of these and make the world more colorful more consistently. Powered by the sales figures and record revenue, the CEO expands the company hires new people. There is a new division to create brighter, longer lasting, quick drying, heat-resistant red paints, another division to create tools to spray paint the bulbs. It is a good time for The Bulb Company. 

# Chapter 7: Innovation 

In distant lands, a group of researchers are still trying to find the filament that can glow red. Remember those filaments that burnt? Those were from this research lab. Their work is mocked at. Their funding is cancelled since they are working on "reinventing the wheel" and all their progress so far is underwhelming with their filaments burning off in less than a minute, though that was still an order of magnitude improvement over those that burnt in seconds. Researchers are not surprised. They rewrite their proposal as creating "longer lasting, brighter red bulbs". They hire a bunch of people from red dyes and also open one group that works on the industry-standard of red-painted bulbs. With this new proposal, they get more funding and the orginal few folks continue their research on the red filament. After 10 years of experimentation the lab produces the world's first red filament that can be used for producing red bulbs.

The news makes headlines. The Bulb Company is quick to notice. It fires all its paints division in a major restructuring. It buys a equipment that can mass produce the red filament. The company produces more bulbs than ever before, with much less bulb failures and also importantly with much less people. 

It is another beautiful and colorful day!
With one bright idea at a time, the innovations continue.
